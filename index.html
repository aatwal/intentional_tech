How has heavy reliance on digital learning since COVID affected SMFCSD students?

Since the pandemic, digital platforms and 1:1 devices have become embedded in everyday instruction, often displacing teacher-led and hands-on learning.

We're concerned parents hoping to work alongside district leaders to make sure technology is used thoughtfully, with an honest look at what we're getting in return for the instructional time, student data, and taxpayer dollars invested.

What we are asking SMFCSD to do

  • Pause student access to generative AI. Wait until we know more and the district is able to responsibly implement it at scale.
  • Conduct an independent technology audit. Evaluate high-use, high-cost, and high-risk tools for learning value, student use, privacy, and cost, and make the findings public.
  • Adopt a clear, lasting districtwide technology policy. Block YouTube, gaming, and other noninstructional content, reduce 1:1 device programs, set screen-time limits by grade, and preserve teachers' professional autonomy in choosing instructional methods.

Questions parents should ask

If a public school is putting a technology or AI product in front of children, parents should be able to ask:

  1. Does this technology actually improve student learning?
  2. What measurable outcome are you expecting: reading proficiency, math achievement, writing ability, attendance, or another clearly defined outcome?
  3. How and when will you evaluate it?
  4. What happens if the results show it isn't working?
Why this matters: Parents and teachers raised concerns about Zearn long before the district changed course. In the meantime, many students were relying on it to build foundational math skills.
