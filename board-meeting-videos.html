Parents, students, and teachers speaking up

Many parents have spoken at San Mateo–Foster City School District board meetings about classroom technology, screen time, and AI. Clips of their public comments are below: 11 clips, most recent first.

Are school-issued Chromebooks helping students learn?

Student voices

"It's too tempting not to use AI if it's right there."
"Everyone knows how to bypass GoGuardian."
"It's distracting when other kids are going online or playing games in class and I'm still doing work."

Teacher voices

Elementary school
"What was wrong with computer labs? Why can't we go back to that?"
"Why do elementary students need access to the internet?"
Middle school
"It's really hard for students to stay focused when they each have a laptop on their desk."
"The kids don't interact as much when they have the choice to use their devices."

Parent voices

  • Concern over students' reduced focus, patience, and willingness to work through difficult material.
  • School devices provide access to YouTube even when families restrict them at home.
  • Google Search's AI Mode is enabled on all student devices, TK–8, rated "Unacceptable Risk" by Common Sense Media.
  • Families want transparency about student-data protection, opt-out information, student performance, and spending.

Six months of parent advocacy

Parents have voiced concerns at school board meetings since March, emailed and met with school and district leaders, submitted multiple public records requests, and engaged local and Bay Area press outlets.

Test scores have declined across nearly all groups.
Explore 11 years of school data
Homework completed

Progress made

  • Superintendent Ochoa announced no take-home Chromebooks for TK–2.
  • Superintendent Ochoa announced a district Technology Use Task Force.
  • Many classrooms are returning to paper, pencils, textbooks, and class discussion.
  • Some schools are restricting devices during lunch and recess.
Teacher's comments
"This is the happiest I've seen them," said one middle school teacher, describing students playing board games together after finishing classwork, rather than turning to their school Chromebooks.
A SMFCSD middle school teacher
